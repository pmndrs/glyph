## Admission finding

**Main and published parity is not demonstrated.** The fixed dense priorities remain materially slower than main and published 0.1.0 in both eight-block control comparisons. The candidate also retains substantial width inspection, sparse borrowing, independent-copy and large prepared-setter gains. Those wins cannot cancel a dense guard failure. The direct E2 comparison gives no dense speedup and no whole-variant nonregression clearance. Preserve the correct input-plan variant and its small favorable observations for a coherent combination, without promoting it as a faster baseline. A native diagnostic count of 12,288 fewer f32 reads per forward/reverse publication proves deleted reads for that fixture, not a shipping Wasm latency reduction. Do not use a 5% minimum to reject a validated small gain, and do not use neutral labels to clear regressions.

## Fixed seven-family guard

1. **Long edits, Unicode/shaping/flow:** Fredoka type-and-measure and cold 22k mounting remain slower against both controls. Plain long type-and-measure is slower than main but faster than release, with a main p95 improvement despite worse p50; do not collapse these phases into one win/loss or infer the specific insertion/removal cause.
2. **Dense preparation/publication:** 512 rewrite and 1024 publish fail p50 and p95 against both controls. This is priority one under the current frontier. Synchronous prepared setters remain a required contract; moving their work to deferred publication is not an admissible parity shortcut.
3. **Width and inspection/materialization:** Width-change inspection and inspection plus publication retain large gains. Cold 22k mounting and cached inspection have separate adverse evidence. The main cached-inspection row is timing-mode mismatched, so its absolute samples do not repair that gap.
4. **Small unchanged setters/ingress:** Large unchanged 8192-span preparation improves markedly. Small first overwrite-before-publication is slower (Labs +53.0% main, +34.4% release), although its absolute cost is sub-millisecond. Two first set-and-measure rows have baseline single-call versus candidate batched modes and cannot establish parity.
5. **Sparse/root physical publication:** Sparse borrow and 1000-label interleaved edit-read publication improve; the latter is 121.403/125.720 to 45.221/46.646 ms pooled p50/p95 versus main. Preserve these gains while assessing dense changes. A count of fewer semantic reads does not prove fewer physical writes or host uploads; this wave's native ledger reports unchanged f32/u32 writes and emitted physical bytes.
6. **Selected copies and inspection:** Independent copy after one edit improves in both comparisons, but copying metrics from 100 unchanged labels is slower (+30.2% main, +84.4% release by Labs block median). Release lacks the two outline fixture rows entirely; that part of this family remains unpaired against release.
7. **Tails, memory, delivery:** Dense and cold p95 regressions above remain. The current Wasm is 1,610,289 raw / 599,723 same-method gzip bytes, +1,042 / +174 versus E2; only the shaper Wasm differs from E2 among 947 shipped dist members. Existing scoped memory/ABI/package checks have their recorded bounds. Physical application peak, browser/hero output and final peer/runtime gates remain open and cannot be inferred from these timing arms.

A report label is not a guard verdict. The published “publication after text change” row is labeled neutral at +1.5% because of Labs' default materiality rule even though its reported 95% CI is +1.0..+1.8%; “rewrite 64 labels” is similarly neutral at +4.8% with CI +2.8..+6.2%. Small, statistically discernible changes may matter to a guard even below 5%, while broad aggregate counts (main 90 faster/29 slower/22 neutral/4 excluded; release 103/23/15/2) are no substitute for family-level parity. Workload medians are separate operations, not an application frame-time distribution.

**Direct-prior decision:** E4 is a correct, preserved **combination candidate**, but not an accepted dense optimization or a cleared nonregression successor to E2. The two priority dense medians and tails are essentially unchanged, with the 1024 raw p50/p95 fractionally worse. Inspection is effectively unchanged, sparse borrow has an adverse but inconclusive p50 and larger empirical p95, and unchanged setter preparation has a higher pooled p95. The independently copied-label row has a small favorable block CI and p50/p95 movement; it is eligible to preserve without a 5% floor. The toggle gain is also observed. Neither establishes that the targeted gather read deletion lowers the fixed dense end-to-end path. Keep E2 independently recoverable, and if a compatible hybrid incorporates E4, evaluate the combined whole variant against E2, main and release on the same required gates. This review does not select the hybrid algorithm.

Labs reports 1 faster, 2 slower and 142 neutral, but “neutral” hides smaller directional evidence. Besides copied-label improvement, 100-label no-op traversal is +4.2% (CI +2.1..+7.6%), measure after text change +1.4% (+0.6..+2.8%), and one-label first set +2.7% (+1.2..+6.1%). Favorable sub-5% examples include 1000-label no-op traversal −2.8% (−6.4..−0.9%), last same-length 1000-label scene publication −2.3% (−4.2..−1.0%), and font-size publication −1.5% (−1.9..−1.1%). The two report-slower rows are 10-label no-op traversal (+23.5%, about 0.73 µs by block medians) and paragraph text mutation (+11.4%, about 53 ns); the report-faster row is 1000-label group toggle (−7.5%, about 33 µs). These tiny or unrelated-path rows are negative controls against attributing every shift to the gather edit. With 145 inspected workloads, unadjusted per-row p-values, sequential sampling and software calibration, a narrow CI is not proof of a direct mechanism. They do prevent a blanket “no regression” statement, and the mixed sparse/unchanged p95 observations should remain visible. No repeat timing is warranted merely to seek a favorable label.

All seven fixed families remain represented in the direct E2 arm. The main/release dense, long-edit, cold, cached-inspection and unchanged-copy deficits above are still unresolved; prior-relative neutrality does not erase them. The next coherent hybrid may use the fully preserved E4 correctness work as an experimental component, with its own exact combined-artifact matrix and downstream gates. It is not yet a performance-accepted base. Physical application peak, browser/hero, final peer/runtime and held-stack readiness remain open.
