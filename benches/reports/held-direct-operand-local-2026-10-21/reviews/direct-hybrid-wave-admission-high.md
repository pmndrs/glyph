## Decision

**Do not promote E5 as the live performance base. Preserve the entire correct E5 variant for one bounded repair experiment; use E2 `28fd0413…` as the latency control and fallback.** The one saved E5 candidate sample was reused byte-identically in three completed eight-block arms. E5 versus E2 has **0 Labs-classified faster, 14 slower, 131 neutral, 0 excluded** among 145 identities. Its dense 512 and 1,024 rows are slower by about 5.8% and 10.1% in the Labs block-median comparison, and the broad 8,192-paint-span paths, font-size publication, color publication and text publication also worsen. The source change is a genuine native write deletion, but it is not an elapsed gain. No 5% minimum is imposed on future gains: even a smaller independently supported gain is eligible for combination. A correct neutral variant remains preserved; overall main/release parity is not a prerequisite for per-wave retention. This particular variant has no validated E2 gain to retain in the active path, while it adds memory, binary size and source complexity.

The E5 matrix also **does not establish parity** with authenticated main `4e25a96a…` or published 0.1.0. The pooled stored-sample p50/p95 for dense 512 are main `7.098/7.876`, release `7.487/8.096`, E2 `9.385/10.538`, E5 `10.019/11.178` ms. Dense 1,024 are `15.397/16.381`, `15.327/17.232`, `18.514/21.048`, `20.359/22.668` ms respectively. Those p95s describe each archive's stored samples; they are **not** independent confidence intervals. Labs' separate block-median rows and CIs appear in the raw `summary.md` files. Main's software clock calibration warning is 10.5%, release's 5.3%; these are not hardware GHz or a license to erase rows. Main has three timing-mode exclusions. Release has five, plus two candidate-only outline identities (143/145); E2 has 145/145, no exclusions and no global warning. All three terminal jobs exited zero and share byte-identical E5 `candidate.json` SHA `700944329123880bcb040be199f39bd938af964247a34581c5e13811981b04bd`.

## Source and cost: why both architectures win somewhere

The main `4e25a96a` planner's `_updateText` resolves/validates desired state and marks `semanticDirty`; `#compileFrame` gathers final dirty content into one publication request. With no intervening query, overwrites before publication can avoid shaping intermediate text. Its query path may prepare speculatively. The current `render-planner.ts::_updateText` calls `#prepareTextCandidate` from each bound setter; that compiles the changed text/style/geometry request and invokes `PlanTransport.prepareParagraph`. In Rust, `TextEngine::prepare_update_inner` validates, prepares and commits a queryable semantic revision before the setter returns. Current publication `#compileFrame` supplies lifecycle/order and consumes prepared semantic state. This scheduling is required by the accepted `retained-prepare-publish` decision. Removing semantic execution across accepted setters would change error timing, prepared queries and split independence. Unrequested serialized measurement remains lazy and is not required at every setter.

Within one setter, source-local work is still selectable. Packed UTF-16 discovery, Unicode preflight, safe shaping windows, retained run/cluster/flow convergence and positioned SoA reduce dirty scope where dependencies close. Arbitrary authored replacement has an input discovery/encoding lower bound; contextual shaping, bidi and changed flow may expand it. `ParagraphState::prepare_clusters`, `prepare_flow_lines` and `prepare_positioned` are real consumers of that state. The sole E5 CPU capture assigns **527/878 samples, 790,207/1,357,000 sampled microseconds** to setter preparation; publication owns **306 samples, 454,502 microseconds**; **45/112,291** are unattributed. The named `set text` node 7 and `scene.updateMatrixWorld` node 44 are disjoint children under the benchmark bulk update; nested matrix node 132 is excluded. This is one sampled CPU capture using the installed archive, 10 warmups, 80 callbacks, 1,024 sets and one scene update per callback, zero timed reads, one draw/9,216 glyphs. Its diagnostic elapsed mean 16.449 ms is not paired with the matrix's 20.359 ms and must not be used as a latency delta. Named preparation self hotspots include HarfRust shaping, positioned/cluster preparation, planner validation and JS request work. Their self samples cannot be summed with inclusive ancestors or declared entirely removable.

The exact producer/consumer seam for E5 is narrower. `CodecGatherWorkspace::append_from_internal` walks 10,240 source rows and resolves selection/program, producing `PlanGlyph`, masks, source correspondence and the selected-row locators. `DirectOperandSource::row` maps each locator to positioned SoA, binding and strike; `DirectRowContext::{f32,u32}` currently constructs semantic field-slice arrays and a `LayoutPlanInput` on **each operand read**, then calls the existing `source_f32/source_u32`. `codec.rs::execute_scalar_records` and `execute_simd_records` load each active program operand through that path, with four direct lane reads assembled into a stack vector in SIMD mode. `plan_packing::execute_run_with_source` feeds the same Codec operation loops; `ordered_plan::write_changed_ranges` still derives active buffers, physical ranges, old/new comparison and patches. The existing packed path keeps contiguous SoA fields and vector loads. Thus direct mode trades packed gather writes and contiguous Codec reads for a 20-byte selected-row locator, indirect positioned/binding reads and repeated row-view construction. The profile has direct `f32` **79,711** and `u32` **23,835** self sampled microseconds across 80 callbacks, `execute_program` **70,290**, and `build_direct_candidate` **30,124**. Those figures are diagnostic upper bounds on any local optimization, not removable elapsed milliseconds; they do not prove how much is field extraction versus view construction or cache locality.

The authenticated Bitmap replay records 6,157 events / 4,099 results / 1,977,392 encoded-result bytes exactly. Per forward or reverse warm publication E5 visits 10,240 source rows, selects 9,216, has zero candidate misses, writes **zero actual** gather f32/u32 fields, stores 184,320 locator bytes, and performs 138,240 f32 + 36,864 u32 direct operand loads. E4 actually wrote 97,280 f32 + 20,480 u32 gathered slots, 117,760 slots / 471,040 bytes; that is the legitimate write deletion. The `elidedFullFieldWrites=175,104` counter is a hypothetical full fresh declaration count, not deleted E4 writes. Comparison still reads 589,824 physical bytes, finds 221,184 equal and emits 368,640; these bytes and accepted renderer ownership remain. The 2,304 four-row eligible groups with no scalar tails prove only native grouping eligibility, not runtime Wasm SIMD frequency. No counter proves net traffic, cache misses, GC or speed. The current E5 source holds both `gather` and `direct_candidate` workspace capacity, consistent with the measured allocator increase but not a complete causal allocation attribution.

Source makes SWAR/packed SoA and rope sharing complementary, not mutually exclusive. SWAR lowers text-scan coefficients; SoA gives Codec contiguous vector reads. Ropes share 32-record clean leaves and stable semantic identity; the final flat physical output and host upload still demand work proportional to changed rows/bytes. SIMD can improve arithmetic but direct lane assembly can erase its input benefit. The data do not establish a maximal implementation, nor justify a new cache, parser, executor, or segmented physical format.

## One next coherent wave: repair the direct row boundary, then decide its retirement

The largest **source-proven avoidable term within E5's changed path** is repeated construction of the same row's positioned semantic slice arrays and `LayoutPlanInput` for every active Codec load, compounded by a separate whole-owner admission walk before `build_direct_candidate`. This is an experimental repair, not a claimed parity fix; the preparation phase is larger overall, but its HarfRust/flow/positioning work has no source-proven complete deletion in this input. Keep E2 as the control/live latency anchor and develop the repair on the preserved E5 variant. Do not use a favorable helper timer as the admission gate.

Ownership: `state.rs::{DirectOperandSource,DirectRowContext,direct_candidate_admissible,build_direct_candidate,prepare_update_inner}`; `codec_gather.rs::{append_direct_from,append_from_internal,source_f32,source_u32}`; `codec.rs::{execute_scalar_records,execute_simd_records}`. `plan_packing.rs`, `ordered_plan.rs`, `render_plan_compiler.rs` should need no structural change; they remain the one physical authority. The TypeScript planner, public API, ABI, persistent 32-record rope and packed field layout should remain untouched. Implementation sketch:

1. Keep the cheap key/checkpoint/compositing/lifecycle/order gates before candidate mutation. Merge the remaining owner-level source-totality/range checks with the candidate's existing active-order traversal. For each owner, establish old source/selected ordinal consistency and declared-domain totality **before** consuming its rows; append the owner via the existing selection/correspondence path. Any late non-mutating admission miss discards only the call-scoped candidate and enters the original packed gather. Real errors and their precedence remain governed by the packed authority; an unexpected certified direct read is an internal invariant defect, not a new public result variant. Do not convert an allocation failure to a silent miss if the packed path would have surfaced that failure first under the accepted error policy.
2. In the scalar Codec loop, build one borrowed row view from the current positioned slices, binding and selection **once per selected record**. In each four-row SIMD group, build four such views **once per group**. Execute the existing operation stream in its existing order; an active `LoadF32/U32` calls the same `source_f32/source_u32` with that view, while stores and finite checks remain unchanged. Hoist only slice-array/view construction and repeated row-coordinate resolution. Do not eagerly read every declared field: program operation order, active-buffer masks, finite checks and source-error precedence must remain intact. Avoid a self-referential stored struct by keeping slice arrays local to the scalar record or four-row group and borrowing them for the loop's lifetime. Preserve the packed `v128_load` route and the direct four-lane assembly; do not duplicate the Codec arithmetic interpreter.
3. If a call transitions from metadata-only direct output to narrow sparse output, rebuild packed columns from the authoritative positioned state exactly as today. Candidate capacity may be reused only while no renderer lease can observe it; release or cap extra retained capacity if the scoped allocator gate still exceeds E2 with no supported speed benefit. Accepted plan state and physical payload publish only after all fallible work completes. Failed admission/abort keeps old accepted generation and independent `split` leases; renderer rejection retains the prepared revision and retries from its checkpoint contract.

Let `O` be owners, `S` source glyphs, `P` selected rows, `L` active Codec loads, `F` declared field columns and `B` physical bytes. Current E5's additional pre-admission walk is `O(O)` plus candidate `O(S)` and per-operand view setup `O(L·F)` for the slice-array construction (source field access itself remains `O(L)`). The proposed call-scoped traversal remains `O(O+S+L+B)`, with row-view setup `O(P·F)` and no new persistent field columns. It does **not** remove the 175,104 direct operand loads, 184,320 locator bytes, 589,824 comparison bytes or 368,640 emitted bytes in the Bitmap witness; claiming those as savings would be false. It targets the extra owner walk and repeated per-load view construction. The memory target is no more than four borrowed views plus existing locator/candidate storage; no new global cache or second prepared layout. If Rust's borrow/lifetime constraints force persistent copies or a second Codec loop, stop this approach rather than hide duplication behind an adapter.

Proof and admission are whole-wave, not per-helper: exact source/lock/archive/named-Wasm authentication; independent cold semantic text/Unicode/bidi/shape/flow/placement/IDs, query and split oracles after each setter; byte-identical cold physical plan/host output after publish; actual Bitmap replay with complete source and field/load/locator/compare/write counters. Cover 0/1/2/3 SIMD tails, recordless rows, strike changes, position-only and zero masks, source-totality with custom unused/missing fields, count-changing front/middle/tail edits, decorations, sparse↔direct↔sparse, order mutation, allocation/overflow, abort, renderer rejection/checkpoint/rebuild, and borrowed lease expiry. Native work counters must prove one owner walk removed and one row view per record/group, with unchanged physical bytes and no broader scope. Then **one** frozen exact-archive full seven-family matrix against main, release and E2 with pooled absolute p50/p95, Labs block-median CIs, every exclusion/mode/calibration warning, plus scoped live/peak/transition memory and raw/gzip Wasm/JS. Final product browser/hero, API/ABI/peers, lifetime and whole-app memory remain separate readiness gates. A partial validated gain may be kept without overall parity; a correct inconclusive repair stays recoverable. If dense and other target rows still offer no validated E2 gain or material tradeoffs remain, retire the *entire* direct branch (`DirectOperandSource`, locator/candidate mode, direct Codec input and threaded `direct_source` parameters) from the active path and restore E2's packed authority; keep the named E5/repaired source for future combinations. This is a real deletion target, not a permanent second execution path.

This wave cannot alone be promised to close a roughly 5 ms main/release dense gap: even the **entire** sampled direct row getter self time is only about 1.29 ms per profiled callback when divided by 80, and sampled CPU is not elapsed time. If the hoisted view removes little work, the precise obstruction is that required source extraction/semantic setter work, not repeated view construction, dominates. The disconfirming check is a source-level count of view construction versus source extraction on the authenticated Bitmap replay, followed by the one whole-operation matrix; do not add a new profile or helper timing loop now. If no complete old path can be deleted under the required error/ownership semantics, stop optimization under this hypothesis and report the contract cost rather than invent a caching or parser shortcut.

## Earlier advice reconciled

The previous E5 zoom-out correctly predicted the possible locality/SIMD and locator/memory costs, preserved the direct variant, and called for the now-completed whole matrix. Its statement that E5 latency was unknown is superseded: E5 has no classified E2 win and has 14 slower rows. The E4 direct-operand proposal correctly identified the full-field writer/Codec consumer and source-totality/error trap; its hoped-for producer write deletion occurred, but whole-operation speed did not. The E3 reset's adverse allocation and dense/sparse results argue against combining E5 with a fresh complete metadata reset now; they do not veto every same-layout hybrid. The accepted preparation decision and earlier reconciliation remain right about one synchronous executor, lazy unrequested summary, independent split, and the need to protect sparse gains. None of the older `d6`, E2, E3 or E4 profiles is used as an E5 latency measurement.
