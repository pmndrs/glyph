## The architecture-level answer

Main is fast on *bulk write then publish* because an ordinary setter updates TypeScript desired state and dirt; `#compileFrame` later gathers final semantic mutations into one Rust frame. An overwritten intermediate value costs no shaping or positioning unless queried. Main's query-first path builds a speculative paragraph measurement on demand, so its fast setter is partly deferred work, not a faster equivalent completed preparation. See `git show 4e25a96a:packages/glyph/src/internal/render-planner.ts` at `_updateText`, `#queryTextRequest`, and `#compileFrame`, and old `engine/state.rs::measure_paragraph_inner/prepare_update_inner`. A query or many interleaved queries pay for speculative request reconstruction, repeated root/identity work, and eventual publication. Main is therefore strongest in dense no-read writes and weaker in sparse edit/read/publication.

HELD is fast when reads and sparse changes follow writes. `render-planner.ts:623–840,1234–1340` prepares each accepted setter through `PlanTransport.prepareParagraph`; `handle-state.ts:1371–1425` writes the request, invokes the one Rust engine synchronously, and adopts or invalidates the preparation. `state.rs:1514–1715` runs the semantic stages without renderer gather; `state.rs:1810–2260` publishes the already prepared revision. `three/text.ts:1438–1825` reconciles scene entries and settles accepted revisions. Thus a later measure, borrow or independent split can consume one authoritative prepared semantic state; the renderer can consume only accumulated dirty owners. Sparse gathered intervals, 32-record retained ropes, stable identities, placement generations, physical byte equality and scoped output preserve clean work (`codec_gather.rs:820–960,1362–1590`; `ordered_plan.rs:1199–1296,1615–1935`). The price is one complete semantic prepare per changed setter, a retained candidate/accepted lifetime, and extra correspondence/physical bookkeeping. Some of that price is required by the accepted contract; some is representation overhead.

These designs fight along **two independent axes**. Scheduling: main can amortize 1,024 edits into one final semantic frame; HELD must answer or throw after every setter. Representation: a rope, correspondence and packed SoA make sparse reuse and SIMD-friendly codec reads cheap, but dense/cold complete changes can pay admission, source walks, per-row metadata and output construction even when little can be shared. A direct operand path tries to remove SoA writes but trades them for indirect semantic reads and a second candidate workspace. Shifting cost between setter, query and publication can make one benchmark attractive while the complete operation loses. No source evidence proves parity impossible under the synchronous contract, but the old dense advantage cannot be obtained simply by moving the same work back to publication without changing the contract.

The week-long variant history supports this two-axis explanation, without making any one experiment a universal verdict. E2 preserves much of the sparse/read improvement but still misses dense main/release parity. E3 added a shared-layout hybrid; its own complete matrix worsened dense 512/1,024 and sparse borrow against its direct prior, so more metadata and a candidate path were not free. E4's source/input-plan work removed source reads but did not establish dense elapsed gain. E5 deletes actual gathered-field writes and still loses to E2. These are different frozen artifacts and runs; their absolute times must not be pooled or subtracted across studies. The [historical architecture and E3 result](/Volumes/Depot/Developer/glyph/.cache/held-rerun-20261019/repo/.agents/docs/reports/final-performance-architecture-plan.md), [E4 evidence packet](/Volumes/Depot/Developer/glyph/.cache/held-gather-input-plan-20261020/evidence/matrix-consolidated-factual-handoff.json), and E5 matrix below establish that optimization at a single layer can move cost to a neighboring layer.

## Intrinsic constraints versus removable conflict

**Intrinsic under the accepted contract.** Each changed setter must synchronously validate, shape/flow/position enough to make its revision queryable, or throw at that call. Complete dense output still requires work proportional to selected rows and emitted bytes. Public query and independent split own snapshot/lifetime obligations. Batch/order, renderer rejection, generation fences, rollback, in-flight bytes and caller-error timing constrain reuse. Rust remains semantic authority; SIMD/SWAR and packed/SoA remain valid local tools. `retained-prepare-publish.md` and `engine-call-contract` define these constraints.

**Removable only with proof.** A setter should not traverse unrelated owners to certify one paragraph; ordinary `prepare_lifecycle` already returns early on no lifecycle change (`state.rs:3812–3841`), while `prepare_semantic_inputs` has a `Single` scope (`3412–3485`) and publication work order derives affected owners (`3343–3373`). Do not claim an O(N²) root scan from these files. There is, however, per-call validation, request wire work and `admit_preparation_owner`'s reserve/search (`state.rs:4391–4413`), and the E5 profile samples these. E5's `direct_candidate_admissible` then walks all active owners before `build_direct_candidate` walks them again (`2960–3195`), and each direct `f32/u32` call recreates semantic slice arrays and `LayoutPlanInput` (`625–710`). This is a specific removable repeated setup, but the operand load, selection, comparison and output remain. Packed SoA has a locality advantage in `codec.rs` while direct access has no proven latency advantage. Rope sharing and contiguous physical buffer writes are not opposed in principle; repeated conversion or metadata for *all changed* rows is optional. A failed direct admission can pay preflight plus fallback, a real cost to count.

## Coherent options, not interchangeable local fixes

1. **E2 packed baseline with existing dense/sparse selection (recommended live base).** Keep E2's single prepared semantic executor, packed SoA gather and one ordered physical compiler. Preserve 32-record retained ropes and narrow dirty intervals for sparse. Dense should use the existing complete physical builder when admitted; no second physical format or executor. A bounded wave would target setter ingress and owner admission only where counters show redundant per-call work, then a separately measured physical stage. Its advantage is low complexity and E2's proven 18.514 ms dense p50; its limitation is still about 3.1 ms behind main and 3.2 ms behind release on the representative dense row. The source has no proved deletion large enough to promise parity. Queries stay prepared, split stays independent, and errors remain synchronous.

2. **Repaired E5 direct operands inside the same ordered compiler.** Fuse the pre-admission and construction owner walk and hoist semantic view construction to once per record/four-lane group, keeping program operation order and exact error precedence. No eager field read or second codec interpreter. This directly addresses measured E5 self work but still retains locators, indirect loads, candidate workspace and packed fallback. The current whole E5 variant is slower than E2, so this is a *bounded falsification experiment*, not the default architecture or a promised parity path. If no complete-operation gain survives size/memory/sparse gates, retire `DirectOperandSource`, `DirectRowContext`, locator/candidate mode and threaded direct-source parameters from active source, while preserving the named whole E5 variant for later combinations.

3. **One canonical owner-to-output stream using existing semantic/layout and ordered authorities.** After synchronous per-owner semantic preparation, expose one call-scoped selected-row iterator with stable identity, mask, binding and positioned SoA references. Let the existing codec's scalar/SIMD operation loop read it and the existing ordered compiler emit packed physical buffers; let sparse owners still use interval/rope correspondence. This could delete a full gathered-field staging pass *and* E5's repeated locator-to-view setup, rather than substituting one for the other. It is genuinely different from a getter hoist: source selection, row metadata, codec operands and output would share one authority. But error order and late missing-field behavior may require a certified totality pass or fallback; if the required double walk and transient ownership recreate E5's cost, reject it. Do not create persistent segmented storage, a second executor or duplicate codec semantics. Its feasible performance is unmeasured and its proof burden is highest.

4. **Explicit deferred bulk transaction (requires user-approved contract change).** A new bulk authoring operation could validate a whole batch and prepare its final state once, preserving a synchronous answer *for that bulk call*, but cannot make each existing individual setter's intermediate revision queryable and throwable at its original call while skipping its semantic work. It may deliver main-like dense amortization for an opt-in API, but would benchmark a different operation and require public API/error/lifetime design and user approval. Do not silently route existing setters through it or call its timing parity with the current dense fixture.

## Recommended implementation brief and deletion ledger

Use **E2 as the active performance baseline**, preserve corrected E5 whole as a recoverable combination candidate, and spend the next compatible wave on *measured per-set and per-owner repeated work* before another source edit is selected. The first implementation gate should instrument existing maintained work attribution on the exact dense fixture to count single-owner request/lifecycle/validation work, owner admissions, paragraph preparations, HarfRust/cluster/positioning changes, selection/source walks, field writes/loads, and physical comparisons/emissions. This is a count/oracle gate, not a new timing profile or helper benchmark. Select one coherent deletion whose old work is counted and whose replacement shares the existing owner/layout/codec authority. I would initially test **one-call-scoped canonical row view** only if it replaces both E5's repeated per-operand view setup and its extra all-owner admission pass; otherwise stay on E2's packed path. Do not promote E5's current direct branch merely because it removed writes. A second wave may target a distinct measured setter cost; do not concatenate speculative microcuts to explain a 3–5 ms gap.

Explicit deletion targets are: E5's duplicate full-owner preflight walk if a certified producer walk can own admission; per-operand semantic slice-array/`LayoutPlanInput` recreation if direct remains; a redundant gathered-field staging pass only if a single codec input authority replaces it with exact cold/sparse output; one transient candidate workspace only when abort/retry ownership proves it unnecessary. If the direct approach fails complete-operation admission, delete its entire active branch instead of retaining dead policy modes. Retain true semantic validation, `prepare_lifecycle` when lifecycle changes, renderer bytes and leases, physical comparison needed for canonical outputs, and the packed SIMD route. No invented cache, Unicode parser, second executor, or unapproved segmented physical storage is part of this brief.

For each candidate, independently compare semantic text, Unicode/bidi/shape/flow/position, measurements, glyph IDs/placement and query/split results against a cold engine after every accepted setter; compare exact physical plan, host payload and renderer pixels/uploads against cold output after publication. Exercise 0–3 SIMD tails, complete and narrow changed intervals, recordless/selection/strike changes, zero masks, decorations, order/batch changes, missing declared sources, arithmetic/allocation faults, abort, renderer rejection/checkpoint, borrow expiry and split lifetime. Count actual producer/consumer work, not hypothetical fields. Authenticate the exact shipping/named Wasm and full archive; then run one frozen candidate across main, release and E2 in the fixed seven-family eight-block matrix, preserving raw p50/p95, Labs CIs, exclusions and calibration warnings. Scope live/peak plus whole-app physical memory, raw/gzip Wasm and JS, API/ABI/peers, browser/hero and final downstream gates separately. Any measured partial gain may be retained as a recoverable whole variant without an arbitrary percentage floor; a correct inconclusive variant stays named, not declared a speedup. Disconfirm the proposed wave if it worsens E2 dense and sparse without a defensible family gain, if the expected work count is not deleted, if a cold/error/lifetime oracle changes, or if memory/size cost overwhelms the measured benefit. Do not infer final parity from neutral classifications or missing baseline rows.

**Feasibility:** E2 is the strongest current dense-compatible held latency control among the preserved variants, but remains materially behind main/release; E5 regresses from it. The profile's largest setter share and source contract imply that a publication-only repair cannot responsibly promise parity. The remaining ~3 ms E2 dense gap may be removable coefficients or the cost of 1,024 synchronous preparations; the available artifacts cannot distinguish those with a rigorous lower bound. If coherent same-contract waves fail to close it, surface the explicit bulk-transaction option as a product/API decision with a changed benchmark contract. Until an exact candidate meets all seven family, correctness, delivery and lifetime gates against both controls, parity and release readiness are **not established**.

## Reconciliation after independent source analysis

The earlier [fresh High zoom-out](/Volumes/Depot/Developer/glyph/.cache/held-direct-retained-20261021/evidence/fresh-sol-high-zoomout-20261020.md) correctly described the scheduling and representation tension but predated the completed E5 matrix; its conditional E5 adoption is superseded. The later [direct hybrid admission review](/Volumes/Depot/Developer/glyph/.cache/held-direct-retained-20261021/evidence/direct-hybrid-wave-admission-high.md) correctly rejects E5 as the live base and identifies repeated direct row setup. I agree with its local repair as a bounded falsification experiment, but I do **not** elevate it to the architecture plan: the profile's setter majority, E2's own dense deficit, and unchanged physical bytes make a direct getter repair insufficiently supported as the parity strategy. The broader owner-to-output stream and the explicit contract-change boundary remain open alternatives with different proof obligations. Historical frontier and master-report timings are tied to their own source/artifacts and are not substituted for the E5 matrix.
